1 · Reading a name to draw a structure

An IUPAC name is a set of instructions. Read it in this order:

  1. Find the parent: the root (meth-, eth-, prop-…) gives the number of carbons in the main chain or ring; cyclo- means a ring.
  2. Find the suffix: it names the main functional group (-ol, -one, -oic acid…) and the locant in front of it says which carbon carries it.
  3. Find the unsaturation: -an- means all single bonds; -en- a C=C double bond; -yn- a C≡C triple bond.
  4. Add the substituents: the prefixes (2-methyl, 3-chloro…) hang off the numbered carbons.
  5. Set the stereochemistry: (R)/(S), (E)/(Z), cis/trans tell you how to draw wedges, dashes and double-bond geometry.
Worked example · 4-methylpentan-2-one
pent- → 5-carbon chain. -an- → single bonds. -2-one → C=O at carbon 2. 4-methyl → CH3 on carbon 4.
Draw C1–C2–C3–C4–C5, put =O on C2 and a methyl on C4. Formula C6H12O.

Current IUPAC names put the locant right before the part it describes: pentan-2-one, but-2-ene, propan-2-ol. Older books write 2-pentanone, 2-butene and 2-propanol. They mean the same structures.

3 · Substituents

An alkyl group is a chain with one H removed, named with -yl: methyl (–CH3), ethyl (–CH2CH3), propyl. Branched groups you will see:

NameStructureAttached through
isopropyl–CH(CH3)2the middle carbon
sec-butyl–CH(CH3)CH2CH3the second carbon of a butyl chain
isobutyl–CH2CH(CH3)2the end CH2
tert-butyl–C(CH3)3the central carbon

Halogens are prefixes: fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo-. Other prefixes: hydroxy- (–OH), amino- (–NH2), oxo- (=O), methoxy- (–OCH3), phenyl- (–C6H5), nitro- (–NO2). Di-, tri- and tetra- count repeated groups: 2,2-dimethyl means two methyls on carbon 2. Prefixes are listed alphabetically in a name, but that never changes the structure you draw.

In aldehydes, acids, esters, amides, acid chlorides and nitriles, the functional-group carbon is C1 of the chain, so no locant is needed: butanal, not butan-1-al. In an ester, the alkyl group named first is the one on the oxygen: in ethyl ethanoate, the ethyl is on O and the ethanoate part has the C=O. An N- locant puts a group on the nitrogen.

Charged species

Carboxylates (ethanoate, CH3COO−), alkoxides (ethoxide, CH3CH2O−) and ammonium ions (methylammonium, CH3NH3+) carry a charge. When you draw one, mark the formal charge and show the lone pairs on the charged atom and on any other heteroatoms (O, N).

5 · Which group becomes the suffix?

When a molecule has several groups, the highest-priority one becomes the suffix and gets the lowest locant; the rest become prefixes.

carboxylic acid > anhydride > ester > acid chloride > amide > nitrile > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > thiol > amine. Alkenes, alkynes, halogens, ethers and alkyl groups never take priority over these.

Worked example · 4-hydroxypentan-2-one
Ketone outranks alcohol, so the ketone is the suffix (-2-one) and the OH is a prefix (4-hydroxy). Five carbons, C=O at C2, OH at C4.

6 · Benzene rings

A benzene ring is drawn as a hexagon with three alternating double bonds; either arrangement of the double bonds is correct. Retained names include phenol (C6H5OH), aniline (C6H5NH2), benzoic acid and benzaldehyde. Positions on the ring are numbered 1 to 6; in common names 1,2 = ortho (o-), 1,3 = meta (m-), 1,4 = para (p-).

7 · R and S

  1. Find the stereocenter: a carbon with four different groups.
  2. Rank the four groups by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog rules: higher atomic number first (Br > Cl > O > N > C > H). For a tie, compare the next atoms out, one sphere at a time; a double bond counts as two single bonds to the same atom.
  3. Point the lowest-priority group (usually H) away from you, on a dash. On the drawing pad, ⟲ Orient does this for you: click a stereocenter and the drawing turns so group 4 points back, without changing R or S. In Practice it also numbers the groups 1–4 and draws the 1 → 2 → 3 arrow.
  4. Trace 1 → 2 → 3: clockwise is R, counterclockwise is S.

If the H is on a wedge (toward you), the direction you see is reversed. Swapping any two groups converts R into S.

Drawing (R)-butan-2-ol
Draw the zigzag C1–C2–C3–C4, put OH on C2 with a wedge, and draw the H on C2 with a dash. Priorities: OH > CH2CH3 > CH3 > H. Check that OH → ethyl → methyl runs clockwise with H in back. If it runs counterclockwise, swap the wedge and dash.

8 · E/Z and cis/trans

For a C=C double bond, rank the two groups on each carbon by the same CIP rules. Z (zusammen, together): the higher-priority groups are on the same side. E (entgegen, opposite): they are on opposite sides. Cis/trans says the same thing when each carbon has one H. On rings, cis means two substituents on the same face (both wedges or both dashes); trans means opposite faces (one wedge, one dash).

9 · Naming a structure

  1. Find the main group (highest priority, section 5). It becomes the suffix.
  2. Find the parent: the longest chain that contains the main group (and as many multiple bonds as possible). If two chains tie, take the one with more substituents.
  3. Number the chain from the end that gives the lowest locant to the main group, then to double/triple bonds, then to the substituents at the first point of difference.
  4. Name and locate the substituents; use di-, tri-… for repeats and list them alphabetically (ignoring di-/tri-).
  5. Add stereodescriptors in front: (R)/(S) for each stereocenter, (E)/(Z) for each stereogenic double bond, or cis/trans for a disubstituted ring.

Grading: a correct name earns 10 points; prefix order, capitals and spacing don't matter, and old-style locants (2-pentanone) are fine. A name that identifies the compound but breaks a rule (wrong numbering, wrong parent chain, a missing locant, a common name) or has wrong or missing R/S, E/Z earns 5. A name for a different compound earns 0.

10 · How drawings are graded

  • Draw skeletal (line-angle) structures. Each line end or corner is a carbon, and carbons carry enough implied hydrogens to make four bonds. Draw heteroatoms (O, N, S, halogens) as letters; their H's are filled in for you.
  • It doesn't matter which way you orient or bend the chain, or in what order you draw the atoms.
  • At a stereocenter named with R or S, draw the H explicitly on a wedge or dash. With the other three bonds spread out in the plane, that one wedge or dash fixes the configuration; you can also wedge or dash a second group.
  • 10 points: right compound with the right stereochemistry. 5 points: right compound, but the stereochemistry is wrong or missing (including a missing H at a stereocenter), or a charged species is missing its lone pairs. 0 points: a different compound.
  • If the name has no R/S or E/Z, any arrangement passes.
